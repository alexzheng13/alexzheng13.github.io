If you develop critical thinking

  1. Fair thinking –> Characteristics: Be strict with yourself, abandon sophistic thinking, and learn to think from others’ perspective –> How to cultivate fair thinking | Consider the opinions you don’t like first | Acknowledge your shortcomings and limitations | Dare to go against the mainstream and challenge your own ideological belief system | Find out the rationale for other people’s opinions before questioning them | Treat others and yourself with the same standards Beware of self-deception such as white lies | The process of good reasoning and clear confidence builds confidence | Pay attention to independent thinking Summary: Resist the influence of external compliance forces and internal self-centered thoughts, and be wary of viewpoints that make you comfortable, that are in line with your own interests, and that satisfy your own selfish needs.

  2. Several stages of becoming an excellent thinker:

  3. Reckless thinker: never thinks about his own thinking process, does not analyze and evaluate his own way of thinking Only make decisions and give feedback to others without serious reflection

    1. Questioning thinkers: able to notice problems in our thinking process Trying to analyze and evaluate your own thinking In exploring those thinking structures that can generate thinking (such as: concepts, assumptions, inferences, meanings, opinions) Consider qualities that optimize thinking (clarity, accuracy, precision, relevance, logic) Discover self-deception in your own thinking
    2. Junior thinker: Analyze the logic of situations or problems Express issues clearly and accurately Check information for accuracy and relevance Distinguish between original information and other people’s descriptions of the event Find out the assumptions that lead to your conclusions Identify biased beliefs, unfair conclusions, incorrectly used words, and ignored meanings Notice when our self-interest affects our perspective Pay attention to two points Dogmatic absolutism - the belief that true ideas are obtained not through reasoning and investigation, but through some preset, irrational beliefs. Subjective relativism - the belief that there are no standards of thinking for judging what is right or wrong.
    3. Thinker in practice: Prepare a plan - test the plan during execution - find an effective plan (not controlled by emotions) Take advantage of “wasted” time Solve a problem every day Internalize thinking standards Keep a thinking diary Practice thinking strategies Reshape your character change your self Change the way you look at things Pay attention to your emotions Analyze the impact of groups on your life

Understand the nature of thinking and the basic mechanism of the mind

Nature of thinking: Self-centered The tendency to be dominated by self-centered thoughts and feelings is one of the most fundamental challenges humans face in learning. Our desires, pains, thoughts, and feelings deeply dominate our lives. We seek self-gratification out of an inherently selfish nature. –> You can start by cultivating a habit. From this process, you can experience the higher level of self-satisfaction brought by small achievements after boring and boring persistence.
